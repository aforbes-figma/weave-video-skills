# Guided Character Intake

Use this reference when the character is uncast, partially specified, or being refined. The goal is a confident collaborative conversation that produces an actionable canon without making the user complete a long questionnaire.

## Conversation pattern

1. **Reflect what is known.** Summarize the role, medium, style, framing, action, and any identity or wardrobe facts already supplied.
2. **Identify the next consequential gap.** Ask only about traits that change silhouette, visible identity, wardrobe construction, expression coverage, or motion.
3. **Recommend before asking.** Offer a default that fits the concept and explain its production benefit in one sentence.
4. **Offer bounded choices.** Give 2–3 useful directions, including the recommendation, and allow free-form correction or “choose for me.”
5. **Update the canon.** Mark the result **exact**, **preferred**, or **free**. Do not re-open approved details unless new shot requirements conflict with them.

Ask no more than three questions per turn. A user should feel directed toward a strong choice, not tested on whether they know production vocabulary.

## Intake order

### 1. Role and downstream use

Establish these before cosmetic detail:

- fictional, stylized, or based on supplied real-person imagery;
- video model and whether references, a trained identity, or both are available;
- close-up, medium, full-body, rear, or multi-angle visibility;
- actions that expose body proportions, footwear, hair motion, rear wardrobe construction, or a signature gesture;
- dialogue, smile, shout, laugh, or other visible mouth states;
- solo or multi-character blocking.

If these are already known from the video plan, inherit them instead of asking again.

### 2. Casting silhouette

Ask for the smallest set that makes the person recognizable at a distance:

- age band or age presentation;
- visual gender presentation, if relevant to casting or styling;
- relative height: short, average, or tall within the intended cast or environment;
- build and proportions: compact, broad, lean, athletic, soft, long-limbed, top-heavy, bottom-heavy, or another user-defined silhouette;
- posture or physical energy when it is a signature part of the character.

Use exact measurements only when matching a real person, preserving scale between characters, interacting with a known set or prop, or choreographing a shot where reach and eyelines matter. Never infer body measurements from a face portrait.

### 3. Face and hair anchors

Ask only for visible, continuity-relevant traits:

- face shape, notable facial structure, skin tone, freckles, scars, moles, facial hair, or makeup when user-established;
- hair color, texture or curl pattern, length, silhouette, part, hairline, bangs, tied or loose state, and facial hair;
- whether hair will be seen from behind or move strongly in wind, dance, running, or action;
- glasses, piercings, hearing aids, headwear, or other identity-linked accessories.

Do not infer ethnicity, gender identity, disability, or other personal identity from appearance, role, name, or voice. If a user wants culturally or historically specific casting, ask directly and respectfully what should be exact.

### 4. Wardrobe construction

Guide the user from large silhouette to small detail:

1. category and era;
2. fitted, relaxed, oversized, tailored, structured, or draped silhouette;
3. layers and front/rear construction;
4. sleeve and hem length;
5. fabric weight, stiffness, texture, and movement;
6. footwear;
7. one or two signature accessories.

Ask about rear construction only when the back will be visible. Ask about fabric behavior when the character runs, turns, dances, sits, encounters wind, or interacts with water. Avoid over-specifying invisible stitching, labels, or materials.

### 5. Performance coverage

Only after the core design is stable, ask whether the pack needs:

- a neutral face anchor;
- a specific smile, teeth, open mouth, shout, laugh, or lip-sync state;
- a profile or rear-hair view;
- a faceless pose or action silhouette;
- a motion reference for gait, dance, gesture, or blocking;
- a second wardrobe state.

Split these into separate references when combining them would shrink or compete with the canonical face.

## How to make suggestions

Base suggestions on story function and generation reliability. Explain the reason briefly:

- “I’d make her slightly tall with a long coat; that gives the character a readable silhouette in the wide shots.”
- “Because he speaks in close-up, I recommend one separate relaxed-smile portrait so the mouth and teeth are not invented during video generation.”
- “The back of the outfit never appears, so I’d leave rear construction free and skip that panel.”
- “The running beat makes shoe shape and trouser fit visible; I’d lock both now rather than rely on the face reference.”

Do not present one recommendation as objective truth. Mark it **preferred** until the user approves it.

## Example guided turns

### Sparse fictional brief

```text
We have the role and visual style, but the silhouette is still open. My recommendation is a tall, slightly long-limbed build because it will read clearly in the planned wide shot.

1. Presentation: feminine, masculine, androgynous, or should I choose?
2. Build: tall and long-limbed (recommended), average and grounded, or short and compact?
3. Wardrobe direction: tailored, casual layered, or workwear?
```

### Dialogue character

```text
The core appearance is locked. Because the character speaks and smiles on camera, I recommend adding one separate close face reference rather than crowding expressions into the body sheet.

Should the delivery use a closed-mouth smirk, a relaxed tooth-showing smile, or a wider comic grin? If the teeth must match a real person, please supply one clear smiling source; otherwise I will mark dental appearance unsupported.
```

### Director-led option

```text
If you want me to cast the remaining details, I’ll choose one coherent direction from the role and mark those choices preferred for your approval before writing the final Weave prompt.
```

## Completion test

The intake is complete when the skill can state:

- what makes the character recognizable in close-up and at a distance;
- which traits are exact, preferred, and free;
- what body range, rear detail, expressions, teeth, hair motion, and wardrobe movement will actually appear;
- which reference owns each required attribute;
- which details remain unsupported and therefore must not be claimed as exact.

Stop asking questions once those decisions are sufficient for the planned shots.
